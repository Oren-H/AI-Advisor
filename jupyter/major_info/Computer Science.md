What you must take for the CS major (SEAS)
- Mathematics requirements
  - MATH UN1101 Calculus I
  - MATH UN1102 Calculus II
  - APMA E2000 Multivariable Calculus for Engineers & Applied Science
  - One Linear Algebra course: choose ONE of COMS W3251 Computational Linear Algebra; MATH UN2010 Linear Algebra; MATH UN2015 Linear Algebra and Probability; MATH UN2020 Honors Linear Algebra; APMA E2101 Intro to Applied Mathematics; or APMA E3101 Applied Math I: Linear Algebra
  - One Probability/Statistics course: choose ONE of IEOR E3658 Probability for Engineers; STAT UN1201 Calculus-Based Intro to Statistics; STAT GU4001 Introduction to Probability and Statistics; or MATH UN2015 Linear Algebra and Probability (may satisfy both the Linear Algebra and the Probability/Statistics requirements—see note). citeturn2view1

Notes and important rules
- Minimum of 62 points for the CS major (includes the ENGI E1006 prerequisite). All major courses must be taken for a letter grade; at most one course (≤4 points) with a grade of D may count. citeturn2view1
- MATH UN2015 Linear Algebra and Probability can simultaneously satisfy both the Linear Algebra requirement and the Probability/Statistics requirement (reduces the number of math courses needed). citeturn2view1
- Overlap limits: no more than one from each of these sets may count toward the major: {IEOR E3658, STAT UN1201, STAT GU4001}; {MATH UN2015, MATH UN2010, APMA E3101, COMS W3251}; {COMS W4771, COMS W4721, STAT GU4241}. At most 6 points of individual research/project/thesis courses may count toward the major. citeturn2view1
- AP credit: a score of 4 or 5 on the AP Computer Science exam gives 3 points of credit and an exemption from COMS W1004. citeturn2view1
- Depth areas/tracks: Formal “tracks” or depth areas applied to the OLD (pre–Fall 2023) curriculum only; they are not part of the current BS requirements. The department’s FAQ labels track guidance as “OLD BS/BA Curriculum,” and the clearance forms distinguish “Spring 2024 and beyond” from “Tracks (Pre 2024).” citeturn4search0turn4search2

Typical semester-by-semester plan (showing ONLY courses required for the CS major; school Core, non-technical electives, Physics, AoE, etc., are intentionally omitted)
Semester 1:
- ENGI E1006 Introduction to Computing for Engineers & Applied Scientists
- COMS W1004 Introduction to Computer Science and Programming in Java OR COMS W1007
- MATH UN1101 Calculus I

Semester 2:
- MATH UN1102 Calculus II

Semester 3:
- APMA E2000 Multivariable Calculus for Engineers & Applied Science
- COMS W3134 Data Structures in Java OR COMS W3137 Honors Data Structures & Algorithms
- COMS W3203 Discrete Mathematics

Semester 4:
- COMS W3157 Advanced Programming
- One of: Linear Algebra requirement (e.g., COMS W3251 or MATH/APMA option) OR Probability/Statistics requirement (e.g., IEOR E3658 or STAT option)

Semester 5:
- CSEE W3827 Fundamentals of Computer Systems
- COMS W3261 Computer Science Theory
- The other Math requirement from Semester 4 (Linear Algebra or Probability/Statistics)
- Area Foundation course (see list below)

Semester 6:
- Area Foundation course (see list below)
- CS Elective (3000+)
- CS Elective (3000+)
- General Technical Elective (GTE) (3000+)

Semester 7:
- Area Foundation course (see list below)
- Area Foundation course (see list below)
- CS Elective (3000+)
- General Technical Elective (GTE) (3000+)

Semester 8:
- CS Elective (3000+)
- General Technical Elective (GTE) (3000+)
- General Technical Elective (GTE) (3000+)

This semester timing matches the SEAS sample plan for when CS core courses are typically taken (1004/1007 in year 1; 3134/3137 and 3203 in year 2 fall; 3157 in year 2 spring; 3261 and 3827 in year 3), while placing Area Foundations, CS electives, and GTEs primarily in the junior/senior years. citeturn2view1
